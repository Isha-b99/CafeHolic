Why we open early

The city is quietest right before it wakes.

Nightjar opens its doors while most places are still dark. No queue, no rush — just the grinder, the kettle, and whoever else is up.

We're named for the bird that sings at dusk and dawn, when almost nothing else is stirring. Same idea, different species.

How the morning runs

The Morning Line

What's coming out of the machine, hour by hour, from first light to closing the pass.

5:30

First pour

Doors open. Filter coffee only for the first half hour — batch brew, no fuss, made for people heading somewhere.

6:15

Espresso bar wakes up

Machine's dialled in, milk's steaming. Flat whites and cortados start moving.

7:00

Pastry drop

The morning bake comes out of the oven — laminated dough, brown-butter loaves, whatever the baker felt like that week.

9:30

The room fills in

Laptops open, regulars claim the window seats. The kitchen switches over to toast, eggs, and the daily soft-serve of gossip.

13:00

Last call, pass closes

We shut early on purpose — this is a morning place. Kitchen closes, but the espresso machine runs till 2.
